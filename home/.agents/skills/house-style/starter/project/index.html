Job queue/retirement/example content

The new queue runs, three big senders are left.

Every service but fourteen sends its jobs to the new queue. Three of the fourteen send nine in ten of the old queue's jobs, and each has a plan.

01/Where it stands

Built, moved, and what is left.

The new queue is finished and most services have moved. What is left sits in three services, so the last moves matter more than their count.

Built

14 clusters run the new queue

9 in production, across 3 regions

  • Retries with a dead-letter queue
  • Quotas for each tenant
  • A dashboard and a backlog alert
  • A replay tool for failed jobs

Moved

38 of 52 services moved

Billing, the first big one, since 4 November

  • 214 of 260 job types off the old queue
  • 9 of 16 cleanup PRs merged
  • Owners asked about 9 idle job types

Left

3 services send 91% of old jobs

Search indexing, Mail and Exports, each with a plan

  • 6 smaller services in progress
  • 5 not started
  • The old queue switches off after the last move

52 services

Done 38 In progress 6 Not started 5 Needs a new path 3

03/Where the jobs are

Three services send most of the old queue's jobs.

Search indexing alone sends over half. Moving these three services ends nine in ten of the jobs the old queue still carries.

04/Next

What happens next, in order.

Each big sender moves on its own date, one at a time, and the old queue stays as the way back until the last one is done.

  1. 4 NovBilling moved. The first big service, with no lost jobs.
  2. 20 NovExports moves. Its plan is approved and its owner runs it.
  3. 2 DecMail moves. After the holiday freeze lifts.
  4. 9 DecSearch indexing moves. Replays first, then live jobs.
  5. 16 DecThe old queue switches off. Read-only for a week, then removed.
